Write a request for feedback to the person described above. This is not a sales pitch: we're asking for help with an early product described in the overview, and they owe us nothing.

## 1. Why them

Two or three specific, verifiable reasons this person is a good fit, referring to their own work (a post, a talk, their role). No generic flattery.

## 2. Outreach message (under 120 words)

Written for the channel they're most active on. It should:

- Open with something specific they did or said that connects to the problem
- Say in one sentence what we're building and the problem it solves
- Make one small, concrete, time-boxed ask (for example, a 20-minute call or trying it for two weeks)
- Say what's in it for them (early access, influence over the roadmap, a direct line to the founder)
- End with an easy out, such as "No worries at all if the timing isn't right"

Avoid feature lists, pricing, "quick demo" language, fake familiarity, and exaggerated praise.

## 3. Follow-up (under 60 words)

A short, friendly nudge to send about a week later if there's no reply. Add one new piece of value, such as a relevant insight, rather than just "bumping".

## 4. Questions for the feedback call

Six to eight open-ended questions: how they handle the problem today, what they've tried, what frustrates them, and what would make them switch, followed by questions about their reaction to the product. Avoid leading questions.

## 5. If they're not a fit

If the research suggests this person isn't a good fit, say so in one sentence and suggest who at their company might be.
